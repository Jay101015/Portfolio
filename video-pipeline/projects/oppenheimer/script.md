[poster src="poster.png" title="Oppenheimer" year="2023" director="Christopher Nolan" runtime="180 min" genre="Biography, Drama" starring="Cillian Murphy, Emily Blunt, Matt Damon, Robert Downey Jr." tagline="The world forever changes" pose=point]
Oppenheimer. Written and directed by Christopher Nolan, based on the book American Prometheus, one hundred and eighty minutes, and shot on film so large the projectors had to be modified to run it.

[still src="ripples.png" search="rain drops puddle ripples" credit="Illustration" caption="The ripples" pose=idle]
{neutral} It opens on rain. Drops hitting a puddle. {point} Nolan has said the whole film is in that image: a chain reaction that starts small and does not stop. By the end, you know exactly what those ripples are.

[list title="Why it works"]
- Cillian Murphy's face does the heavy lifting
- Two timelines, two film stocks, one argument
- The sound is the special effect
- Robert Downey Jr. finally gets to act again
{point} Four reasons this works. One, Cillian Murphy's face does the heavy lifting. Two, two timelines in two film stocks, telling one argument. Three, the sound is the special effect. {happy} And four, Robert Downey Junior finally gets to act again.

[clip src="trinity.mp4" fallback="trinity.png" search="trinity atomic test 1945" credit="Trinity test, United States Department of Energy, public domain" caption="Trinity" pose=shocked]
Trinity. {shocked} The test in the desert. Nolan built it with real explosives, real fuel, real light, {surprised} and the thing that gets you is not the fireball. It is the silence. The flash comes, everyone stares, and the sound does not arrive for nearly a minute. {think} Because that is how far away they were.

[stat value="7" label="Academy Awards" sub="Including Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Actor for Cillian Murphy." source="96th Academy Awards" pose=crossed]
And here is the number. {surprised} Seven Oscars. Best Picture. Best Director. Best Actor for Murphy. Best Supporting Actor for Downey. {smug} For a three hour film where the climax is a committee hearing in a small room.

[still src="hearing.png" search="senate hearing room 1950s" credit="Illustration" caption="The small room" pose=think]
{think} Which brings me to the black and white. Every scene in black and white is the Strauss hearing, the only part of the film not seen through Oppenheimer's eyes. {point} Nolan had Kodak make black and white large format film stock just for this. Nobody had shot on it before. {smug} It is a flex, but it is a flex that tells you whose story you are in.

{angry} Now, what does not work. The women in this film get almost nothing to do, and Florence Pugh deserved better. The last hour is a lot of hearing. {facepalm} And you genuinely need a chart to keep the physicists straight. {happy} But against that. Murphy. The sound design. The Trinity sequence. {laugh} And a Downey performance that reminds you why he was famous before the suit.

[still src="barbenheimer.png" search="cinema crowd summer 2023" credit="Illustration" caption="Barbenheimer" pose=laugh]
{laugh} And we have to mention the summer it came out. Same weekend as Barbie. Pink on one screen, the atom bomb on the other, and people did both in one day. {happy} The two films together made over two billion dollars, and the serious one made nearly a billion of it. {smug} In black and white. About a hearing.

[quote by="J. Robert Oppenheimer"]
> Now I am become Death, the destroyer of worlds.
{point} There is a line everyone knows, borrowed from the Bhagavad Gita, and Murphy barely raises his voice to say it. Now I am become Death, {neutral} the destroyer of worlds. The film is three hours of a man realising what that sentence means.
