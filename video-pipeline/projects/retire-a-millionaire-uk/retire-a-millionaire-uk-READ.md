And I know a million sounds like a lottery number. It did to me. But when you actually break it down, it's a monthly direct debit and a lot of patience. And most of it isn't even your money.

**The number**

Right, the number first. I've assumed your money grows about 7% a year, which is roughly what a global tracker has done over the long run. It's not a promise, some years are horrible. **Who pays**

But 329 quid a month is still a lot when you're 25. So here's where it gets cheaper.

Say you're on 35 grand. Your workplace pension already puts in around £192 a month. But only about £96 of that is yours. Your employer puts in about £72, and the taxman adds about £24. Then you top up the gap yourself, and the taxman adds to that as well. So for £329 going in, about £206 comes out of your pay. The other £123 is free money.

So the cheapest way, by a mile, starts with the pension at work. If your employer matches more when you put in more, take every pound of it before you do anything else. Nothing else you can buy gives you that.

And if 329 feels like too much right now, there's another way to do it. Start lower, and put it up a bit every year. If you raise it by 3% a year, basically every time you get a pay rise, you can start at about £223 a month and still get to the same place.

That's the trick I actually like. You never see the money, so you never miss it. Most workplace pensions let you change the percentage in about two minutes.

After that, it's a stocks and shares ISA. You don't get anything on the way in, but there's no tax at all on the way out, and you can get at it whenever you need to. You can put in up to 20 grand a year, which is more than most of us will ever manage.

**Starting later**

But what if you're not 25. What if you're 35, or 45, and you're looking at that chart thinking, well, that's me done.

It's not done. At 45, a million needs about sixteen hundred a month, which is a lot. But half a million needs about £800. And if your employer's paying in, a good chunk of that isn't yours either.

And you're not starting from nothing. The full State Pension is £241.30 a week right now. That's about twelve and a half grand a year on top of whatever you've built, as long as you've got enough years of National Insurance.

So half a million, plus the State Pension, is still a really comfortable retirement. Starting late doesn't mean you've failed. It just means the number's different.


**What ruins it**

Now the bit that ruins it. And it's not a crash.

It's fees. Same money in, same 42 years. But if you're paying 1% a year more in fees, you don't end up with a million. You end up with about 746 grand. That's a quarter of a million pounds, gone, for a number most people never look at.

So if you do one thing after this video, log in to your pension and find the fee. A lot of old workplace pensions are sat in expensive funds and nobody's ever checked.

The other thing that ruins it is you. Well, me. The market will drop, probably by a lot, probably more than once in 40 years. The 7% only works if you're still in when it comes back.

What helps me is not looking. I check mine about once a month now. When I was checking it every day, every red day felt like an emergency, and that's when you do something stupid.

So that's the whole thing, really. Take the employer money first. Then an ISA, in one cheap tracker. Check the fee. Put it up when your pay goes up. And don't sell when it drops. None of it's clever. It's just hard to keep doing for 40 years.

And I've got to be honest about one more thing. A million in 42 years isn't a million today. With prices going up about 2% a year, it buys what roughly 435 grand buys now. That's still a really good retirement. It's just not a yacht.
