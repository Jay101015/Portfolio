Welcome back. Today we are going back to nineteen sixty eight, {point} to a black and white movie shot by a bunch of friends in Pennsylvania, {surprised} that accidentally became public domain {smug} and permanently changed horror.

[poster title="Night of the Living Dead" year="1968" director="George A. Romero" runtime="96 min" genre="Horror" starring="Duane Jones, Judith O'Dea" tagline="They won't stay dead." pose=point]
Night of the Living Dead. Directed by George Romero, ninety six minutes, black and white, and made for roughly one hundred fourteen thousand dollars.

[clip src="clip1.mp4" from=0 credit="Night of the Living Dead (1968), public domain" caption="The opening" pose=idle]
It opens in a cemetery. Barbra and her brother Johnny are visiting a grave, and within three minutes {confused} the movie has already broken its own rules. {shocked} The slow man in the distance is not a mourner.

[still src="still1.jpg" credit="Night of the Living Dead (1968), public domain" caption="A farmhouse, seven strangers, one night" pose=think]
From there it is one location. A farmhouse, seven strangers, and a night that keeps getting worse. {think} The movie never explains the dead. {shrug} It just lets you watch people disagree about what to do.

[avatar headline="Why it still works" sub="Three reasons" pose=point]
So why does a sixty year old low budget movie still work? Three reasons.

[list title="Why it still works"]
- The dead are slow, the people are the problem
- Duane Jones in the lead
- No hero music, no comfort
One, the dead are slow. The people are the problem. Two, Duane Jones gives a lead performance that the movie never comments on, and that silence says more than a speech would. Three, there is no hero music and no comfort. The ending lands like a slap.

[clip src="clip2.mp4" from=2 credit="Night of the Living Dead (1968), public domain" pose=shocked pos=corner-left]
And that ending. I will not spoil it, but if you think you know how a nineteen sixties horror movie ends, you do not know this one.

[compare title="What works and what doesn't"]
left: What doesn't | | Barbra is written as furniture; Some acting is community theatre; The middle sags
right: What works | | Relentless pacing; The ending; The radio and TV broadcasts
{angry} Now, what does not work. Barbra spends most of the movie as furniture. {facepalm} Some of the supporting acting is community theatre. And the middle stretch sags. {happy} But against that, the pacing is relentless, the news broadcasts build the world for free, {laugh} and that ending.

[stat value="$114,000" label="Budget" sub="Shot in Evans City, Pennsylvania by a crew of first-timers" source="Widely reported production budget" pose=crossed]
One hundred fourteen thousand dollars. That is the whole budget. Shot in Evans City, Pennsylvania, by a crew who mostly made commercials.

[avatar headline="The copyright mistake" sub="Why you can watch it free, legally" pose=facepalm]
{point} And here is the part everyone loves. The distributor changed the title at the last minute {facepalm} and left the copyright notice off the new prints. {neutral} Under the law at the time, that meant the film went straight into the public domain. {sad} Romero never saw most of the money, {laugh} and that is why you can watch it, and clip it, for free.

[score value="8.5" max="10" label="The verdict" verdict="Rough edges, perfect bones. Still the blueprint." pose=thumbsup]
{think} My score. {thumbsup} Eight point five out of ten. Rough edges, perfect bones, and still the blueprint every zombie movie is built on.
