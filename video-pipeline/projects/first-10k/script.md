[list title="The order of operations"]
- Starter emergency fund
- Kill any debt above roughly eight percent
- Grab the full employer match
- Invest the rest in broad index funds
The plan is not complicated. It is an order. Step one, a starter emergency fund. Step two, kill any debt above roughly eight percent. Step three, grab the full employer match. Step four, invest the rest in broad index funds.

[stat value="£2,000" label="Step 1: starter emergency fund" sub="one month of bare-bones costs" source="Illustrative"]
Step one. Not six months of expenses yet. Just one month of bare-bones costs, parked in an easy-access savings account you can reach in a day. This is the thing I did not have, and it is what stops a flat tyre becoming credit card debt.

[compare title="Step 2: why expensive debt comes first"]
left: Credit card at 24% | 24% | Guaranteed cost; Compounds against you

Step two is debt. If you are carrying a credit card at twenty four percent, paying it off is a guaranteed twenty four percent return. No investment gives you that with certainty. So anything above roughly eight percent gets cleared before you invest a pound.

[chart bar title="Step 3: the employer match is free money" y="%" source="Illustrative: a 50% match on 6% of salary"]
bars: Your contribution:6, Employer match:3, Instant return:50
Step three. If your job offers a pension match, take all of it. A fifty percent match on six percent of your salary is an instant fifty percent return on that money. Nothing else in finance pays that on day one.

[chart line title="Step 4: £10,000 at 7% a year" y="£" x="Year" source="Compound growth at 7% a year, illustrative"]

Step four is where the boring part becomes the exciting part. Left alone in a broad index fund at a seven percent average return, ten thousand becomes about seventy six thousand in thirty years. You did not add a single extra pound. Time did the work.
