[title subtitle="The order of operations that actually works"]
What should you actually do with your first ten thousand dollars? Not the fantasy version. The real, boring, works-every-time version.

[stat value="$10,000" label="Sitting in checking" sub="Earning roughly nothing" source="Illustrative"]
Most people get here and freeze. So the money sits in a checking account, earning close to nothing, while they wait for a plan to show up.

[text headline="The plan"]
Here is the thing. The plan is not complicated. It is just an order. And once you know the order, every dollar knows where to go.

[list title="The order of operations"]
- Starter emergency fund
- Kill any debt above roughly 8%
- Grab the full employer match
- Invest the rest in broad index funds
Step one, a starter emergency fund. Step two, kill any debt above roughly eight percent. Step three, grab the full employer match. Step four, invest the rest in broad index funds.

[stat value="$2,000" label="Step 1: starter emergency fund" sub="One month of bare-bones expenses, in a high-yield savings account" source="Illustrative"]
Step one is a starter emergency fund. Not six months of expenses yet. Just one month of bare-bones costs, parked in a high-yield savings account where you can reach it in a day. This is what stops a flat tire from becoming credit card debt.

[compare title="Step 2: why high-interest debt comes first"]
left: Credit card at 24% | 24% | Guaranteed cost; Compounds against you
right: Index fund at 7% | 7% | Long-run average; Not guaranteed
Step two is debt. If you are carrying a credit card at twenty four percent, paying it off is a guaranteed twenty four percent return. No investment gives you that with certainty. So anything above roughly eight percent gets paid off before you invest a dollar.

[chart bar title="Step 3: the employer match is free money" y="%" source="Illustrative, 50% match on 6% of salary"]
bars: Your contribution:6, Employer match:3, Instant return:50
Step three. If your job offers a retirement match, take all of it. A fifty percent match on six percent of your salary is an instant fifty percent return on that money. Nothing else in finance pays that on day one.

[chart line title="Step 4: $10,000 at 7% a year" y="$" x="Year" source="Compound growth at 7% annual, illustrative"]
data: Index fund = 0:10000, 5:14026, 10:19672, 15:27590, 20:38697, 25:54274, 30:76123
Step four is where the boring part becomes the exciting part. Left alone in a broad index fund at a seven percent average return, ten thousand dollars becomes about seventy six thousand in thirty years. You did not add a single extra dollar. Time did the work.

[stat value="$76,123" from="$10,000" label="30 years later" sub="Same $10,000, no extra contributions" source="Compound growth at 7% annual, illustrative" count="3"]
Seventy six thousand dollars. From one decision you made once, and then left alone.

[quote by="Old investing proverb"]

There is an old line about this. The best time to plant a tree was twenty years ago. The second best time is now.

[list title="Do this week" unnumbered]
- Open a high-yield savings account
- List every debt with its interest rate
- Check your employer match
- Open a brokerage account
So here is your homework this week. Open a high-yield savings account. List every debt you have with its interest rate. Check what your employer match actually is. And open a brokerage account, even with nothing in it yet.
