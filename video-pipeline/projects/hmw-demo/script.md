[hook board="A 1% fee costs you £100,000" art="fee-slice"]
One percent. That is what your pension provider takes every year. It costs you about a hundred thousand pounds.

[title kicker="The fee you never see" subtitle="1% a year, 40 years, a third of your pot"]
Here is how a number that small does that much damage, and the three things that stop it.

[text board="It never shows up as a bill" clip="person opening front door at night"]
Nobody sends you a bill. It comes out of the growth, quietly, before you ever see the number.

[stat value="£100,000" label="what 1% a year takes over 40 years" sub="on £420 a month, growing at 7%"]
Four hundred and twenty pounds a month, forty years, seven percent growth. The fee takes about a hundred thousand of it.

[takeaway title="Fees compound too" n="1" of="3"]
Takeaway one. Fees compound, exactly like returns do, only in the wrong direction.

[text board="Every pound taken stops growing" art="snowball-push"]
Every pound the fee takes is a pound that never grows again. Forty years of that is the whole snowball.

[text board="Here's the catch: it's a share, not a sum" art="sam-thinking"]
Here is the catch. The fee is a share of your pot, so the bigger your pot gets, the more it takes.

[takeaway title="Check the number" n="2" of="3"]
Takeaway two. Find the fee. It is on the statement, usually in the small print, as a percentage.

[text board="Find it on the statement" art="pension-letter"]
Look for the annual charge, the ongoing charge or the total expense ratio. Anything over half a percent is a question.

[bite value="£300,000" lose="£100,000" label="A 1% fee, 40 years" source="illustrative"]
Three hundred thousand pounds becomes two hundred. That is the bite.

[takeaway title="Move it, once" n="3" of="3"]
Takeaway three. Move it, once. A global index fund charges a tenth of a percent.

[text board="What ignoring it looks like" clip="crowd rushing busy street"]
And no, ignoring it is not a plan. The fee does not get bored and leave.

[text board="0.1% instead of 1%: £90,000 back" art="sam-pleased"]
Go from one percent to a tenth of a percent and about ninety thousand pounds of that money stays yours.

[recap title="The fee"]
- Fees compound too
- Check the number
- Move it, once
So. Fees compound too. Check the number. Move it, once.
