There are three things that make a pension far cheaper than it looks. Lever one: your employer. By law, if you're enrolled in a workplace pension, your employer has to put in at least three percent of your qualifying pay, as long as you put in your share. Plenty put in more, and some will match whatever you add, up to a limit. If you're not taking the full match, you're leaving part of your pay on the table. Lever two: tax relief. Money you put into a pension comes out before income tax. So for a basic-rate taxpayer, one hundred pounds in your pension only costs you eighty pounds. The other twenty pounds is tax you would have paid anyway. For a higher-rate taxpayer, that same one hundred pounds can cost as little as sixty pounds. And when you retire, you can usually take up to a quarter of your pot tax-free. Which means the tax on your retirement income is lower than our numbers assumed. That works in your favour.
