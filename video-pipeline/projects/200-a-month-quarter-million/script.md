[hook board="£200 a month → £244,000" art="payday"]
Two hundred pounds a month. That's it. Leave it alone for thirty years, and it could turn into almost a quarter of a million pounds.

[title kicker="The Everyday Investor" subtitle="Why small amounts win"]
Here's how that actually works.

[broll search="british pound notes" caption="Cash is shrinking" src="03-british-pound-notes.mp4" credit="Video 295411 by DEZYNE_3D via Pixabay"]
First, the bad news. Money sitting in your current account is shrinking.

[stat fact="uk.cpi" sub="the same tenner buys a little less" value="3.5%" source="ONS, September 2026" label="UK inflation (CPI)"]
Every year, prices go up, and the same tenner buys you a little bit less.

[text board="Invest it, don't park it" art="investing-app"]
So instead of letting it sit there, you put two hundred pounds a month into a low-cost global index fund, inside a stocks and shares ISA, so you never pay tax on the growth.

[grow from="£200" per="month" years="30" rate="7%" label="£200 a month, 30 years" source="7% a year, the market's rough long-run average; illustrative"]
Over thirty years, you'd pay in seventy-two thousand pounds. But if the market grows at around seven percent a year, which it has roughly averaged over the long run, that pot becomes around two hundred and forty-four thousand. Most of that is growth, not your money.

[takeaway title="Time beats amount" n="1" of="1"]
The secret isn't how much you invest. It's how early you start, and how long you leave it.

[recap title="The short version"]
- Cash loses value every year
- £200 a month, invested, adds up
- Start early and leave it alone
