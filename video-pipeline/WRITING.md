# Writing for retention

Read this before drafting any script, writing a recording brief, or giving a draft the editor's pass. The editor
never changes a spoken word (EDITOR.md, hard rules), so most of this has to be in the script before Jeffrey records.
Where the recording already exists, the editor's pass reports what is missing (see "The editor's check" below)
and the courier tells Jeffrey, so the next recording has it.

None of this is a trick. Finance videos rarely say anything new; people stay for how it feels to watch. These are
the craft rules that make the same facts feel worth ten minutes. Honest packaging only: no fake urgency, no "stay
to the end for a secret", no title the video does not deliver. The audience is people making a careful decision
with their money, and they trust a channel more for not doing that.

## The rules

1. **Open loops.** Promise something specific early and pay it off later. Plant two or three in the first minute
   ("the one cost nobody tells first-time buyers about, in a minute"; "I'll show you exactly where I land") and
   close each one, in order, later in the video. A loop that never closes is a broken promise: every one is paid off.
2. **Stakes before facts.** A number only lands when the viewer feels what it costs them. Every big number gets a
   "which means for you..." sentence: "£1.2M in Kensington" is trivia; "£1.2M means on £250K a year you still
   can't afford Kensington" is a feeling.
3. **But and therefore, never and-then.** Beats join with a turn: here's the rule, BUT here's the catch, THEREFORE
   here's what I'd do. A script that goes fact, fact, fact leaks viewers; each turn makes a small tension and
   resolves it. ("Three ways to stretch, each with a catch" is the shape to copy.)
4. **A re-hook every 60 to 90 seconds.** Attention resets. Each new section opens with a line that promises the
   next bit is worth staying for: "and this is where it gets worse", "here's the bit that changed my plan",
   "now the number that actually decides it". Takeaway cards (README.md) are the on-screen form of the same thing.
5. **Pay off inside the first minute.** One genuinely useful thing before the title card ("banks lend about 4.5
   times your salary: multiply yours, add your deposit, that's your budget"). It proves the video is worth the
   time and buys the next nine minutes. The hook rules in EDITOR.md still apply: the first line is the number,
   never a greeting.
6. **Specific beats round.** "£2,170 a month" is more believable and more memorable than "about £2,000". Keep the
   odd numbers; they show the work was done.
7. **Jeffrey is the story.** "I'm house hunting too" is the channel's biggest retention asset. The same facts from
   someone in the middle of the problem are a story; from a stranger they are a lecture, and people finish stories.
   Give his own situation screen time: his salary, his deposit, the town, what the agent said, what he decided.
8. **Name the viewer's doubt.** "You're probably thinking that's impossible on £25K." Saying what they are thinking
   keeps them in the conversation. It costs nothing.
9. **Plain and warm.** "Income multiple" loses people; "how many times your salary the bank will lend" does not.
   One idea a sentence; no jargon the viewer would have to look up.
10. **End with momentum.** The last 30 seconds are the summary and one clear next step ("work out your number
    tonight"), then the watch-next card. A video that trails off is skipped at the end, which drags average view
    duration down.

## The opening, in three sentences

Written before recording, every time. Sentence one: the single most surprising number. Sentence two: why it
matters to the viewer (the stake). Sentence three: what they will be able to do by the end (the promise, and the
first open loop). Then the title card. Footage behind the opening where the sentence names something you can film.

## The editor's check

The editor's pass (EDITOR.md) reads the draft against this list and writes what is missing into the courier's
notes for Jeffrey, never into the narration:

- `WRITING: no open loop in the opening` / `WRITING: loop "<promise>" never paid off`
- `WRITING: big number without a stake sentence (scene N: "<number>")`
- `WRITING: <N> seconds between re-hooks (scenes A to B)` when a gap passes about 90 seconds
- `WRITING: nothing useful before the title card`
- `WRITING: the ending trails off (no next step before the watch-next)`

These are reported on every build and read against the retention curve at 48 hours and 7 days (LEARNINGS.md), so
each rule earns its place or goes.
