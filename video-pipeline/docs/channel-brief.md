# The Everyday Investor: channel brief

Jeffrey's handoff, 3 November 2026. Read this before planning or writing a video.

## The channel
- Jeffrey, 27, London. Faceless UK finance and investing channel, @TheEverydayInvestor1, narrated in his own voice.
- Started late October 2026. Audience so far about 75% UK, men 25 to 44.
- Goal: monetised before February 2027. Today that is 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 long-form watch hours in a year.
  From 1 February 2027 new applicants need 8,000 hours (or 20 million Shorts views in 90 days); widely reported,
  check YouTube's own announcement. Hours earned before then still count, so the push is now.
- Then 200K views a month, aiming for 500K a month within about 10 months.
- Benchmark: British Finance with Jack (UK, faceless, about 12K views a video, posts daily). Goal: outpace him.
- Plan: 3 long videos a week (maybe 4), 3 to 6 Shorts per long video. Shorts are for subscribers. YouTube only.
- Acting is Jeffrey's main goal; this channel is something he enjoys. Pace it so it stays that way.

## How Jeffrey wants scripts
- Always two files: READ (spoken words only, to record) and STUDIO (tagged, for the pipeline).
- Natural spoken language that sounds like him, with cleaner structure. He never says "quid".
- The finished video must be 10 minutes or more. He cuts while recording (a ~1,900-word script became 7:22),
  so write about 2,800 words to land 10 to 11 minutes after cuts.
- Frame around the viewer as an individual ("you", "your salary").
- Every UK figure gets a source and a month. His own numbers stay as [BRACKETS].
- Brutally honest feedback; flag confidence on anything unverified; include links.

## What the analytics have taught us (November 2026)
1. Titles: search-style, about the viewer, with a money number. Two stalled videos only moved after retitling:
   "How Much House Can I Afford…" (48 to 200+ impressions; browse 29% to 67%) and "Our Parents Had It Easier…" to
   "You'd Need £83,000 a Year to Live Like Your Parents (UK)" (11 to 738 impressions, 5%+ click-through).
2. Click-through of 4 to 6% on browse traffic is healthy. A high rate on tiny impressions means nothing.
3. Best opening so far, the £83,000 video: about 60% still watching at 0:30. Number comparison in line one, a
   stakes twist, a stat scene, specific teasers ending "at the end, my honest plan", title card at about 25 s,
   never "in this video".
4. "I Bought the Best Stock Every Year…" got the biggest first test (1K impressions in 2 days, 96% browse) but
   lost about half its viewers in the first 40 seconds (1:12 average of 5:58). Packaging works; the opening must
   hold like the £83,000 one. Likely cause: the hook says the title's answer (£30) in the first sentence.
5. Re-watch spikes happen on real screens (his Trading 212 pie). Show real numbers more.
6. Mid-roll ads need 8 minutes or more; aim for 10+.
7. Pay off the title's number late (EDITOR.md); keep the video on the title's question.
8. B-roll is useful (Jeffrey): aim for about one scene in three as footage, one in four at the least.
