# Pose library: the character for every situation the channel covers

The videos cut between held poses of one character on the words of the voice (an animatic, the way the reference
Short does it). This is the list of poses to build up to, grouped by situation, so any script on any money topic
has a pose for its moments. One character throughout (curly dark hair, a plain long-sleeved top with the sleeves pushed up, jeans with
turned-up hems, trainers: library/poses/source/batch-0-sheet.png is his sheet), drawn from that sheet.
Every pose is flipped left to right for free, so each line here is two usable drawings.

★ marks the core 40, made first. Each video then adds about 8 for the moments it had no good pose for, so the library
passes 100 on what the scripts actually need. Poses are made in batches of 8 per image (one strip keeps the
character consistent), cut with `scripts/flipbook-cut.py`, and tagged so the pipeline can pick one for a word.

Status: `have` = cut and in the library, blank = still to make.

## How he moves (the house style)

Like Pingu: stop motion with bounce, in black ink.

- Everything moves on twos: 12 changes a second, never smooth.
- A new pose lands with squash and stretch (wider and shorter, then taller and thinner, then settled) and the words
  that appear with it land the same way.
- A small dip in the two frames before the next pose (anticipation).
- Happy poses (thumbs up, fist pump, arms up, celebrate) land with a little hop.
- He jiggles slightly while holding, a new tiny offset every other frame, like clay touched between frames.
- Walking: the walk cycle held 3 frames a drawing, moved one step's worth per drawing, a light rise off each step.
  **No waddle** (no side-to-side rock). He walks on and off instead of sliding.
- Next to come: 2-3 in-between drawings for the actions used most (thumbs up, point, wave, shrug, fist pump,
  facepalm, jump, sit down, hand over) so an arm travels into a pose instead of snapping there.

## Supporting cast (later)

Some moments need a second person: an estate agent, a landlord, a boss, a bank clerk, a partner or friend. Once the
main library has its core 40, give each of three supporting characters their own sheet and 8 poses, drawn to stand
beside him in the same style.

## Making a batch

1. The character sheet first (front, three-quarter and side), kept as the reference for every batch.
2. Eight poses per image, one row or a 4×2 grid of equal panels, white gaps between, each pose described in order.
   Same size, same ground line, black marker line art on white, no shading, no colour, no text.
3. Check: the same face, hair and clothes in every panel; hands with five fingers; nothing cut off.
4. Cut, tag in the manifest, mark `have` here.

### Backgrounds (one batch, no people)

Simple line-art rooms and places with nobody in them, a clear floor in the middle third for the character to stand
on: a bank branch; an estate agent's window and desk; a living room; an office desk; a supermarket aisle; a train
platform; a high street; a kitchen. Same marker style, lighter lines, no text, no signs with words.

### On a Short

`shorts.json` picks name poses on words (`"poses": [[seconds in the long video, "fist-pump"], ...]`); the render
draws them with remotion/src/paper/poses.tsx from remotion/public/poses/solid/ (each pose filled with white paper, made
by scripts/pose-cut.py). In-betweens are found by their `into` in the manifest.

### Sizing him in a room

Never one size for every room. Measure each background once, against something of known height standing at the
depth where he will stand (a kitchen counter 0.9 m, a front garden wall 1 m, a door 2 m, a for-sale post 2.4 m):
from two such things find the eye line, then his height on screen is (floor y - eye line) x 1.75 / 1.6. Put him where
the floor is open at that depth; a table can stand in front of him only if his feet are behind its back edge on
screen, otherwise he looks as if he is standing on it. A room drawn very close (the living room) is cropped higher so
he fits.

Each measured room keeps its spot in library/backgrounds/manifest.json (`spot`: x, floor and h as shares of the
drawing; `land_top` where the landscape crop was chosen by eye; `walk_from`/`walk_dx`, the side he can walk in from
without crossing anything nearer the camera). pipeline/setpiece.py turns the spot into both frames: the landscape crop
(the full width, a 16:9 band, his feet above the captions at 0.80) and the Short's 9:16 slice (him about two thirds
across, the cards beside him, his feet above the Shorts title at 0.80 and his head under the captions at 0.26,
zooming in a little when the spot needs it). Check a room, or try a new spot, with
`python3 scripts/room-stage.py <room> [--x --floor --h]`: it draws him in both crops with the caption and Shorts
zones. Measured so far: estate-agent, first-flat, living-room, office (the New York tower painted out of its window),
terraced-street. A room without a spot cannot be used in a [set] scene until it is measured.

### Set pieces (whole-scene moments)

B-roll is the main picture; the character gets two or three set pieces a video, each using a whole background:
walk in, react on the word, act it out, leave. In a script:

    [set room=first-flat walk cards="£12,000 deposit; £125,000; ink:£600 a month; 3 years -> 2 years 9 months"]
    Add a {count-notes} £12,000 deposit and your budget is around {fist-pump} £125,000. {prop=keys} That's around
    £600 a month, and three years becomes {point-board} two.

- `{pose}` before a word: he lands that pose on it (in-betweens first, a hop on happy poses; `{jump}` is crouch,
  leap, landing). `{pose:flip}` mirrors it. point-board points at the cards by itself.
- `{prop=keys}`: a prop from library/props pops into the card column on that word.
- `cards=`: each lands on the word its figure is said ("3" also matches "three"); `@word` picks the word; `red:`,
  `green:`, `ink:` the colour (figures green, words ink); `old -> new` strikes the old one through. With no cards=,
  every amount said gets a card; `cards=off` for none.
- `walk`: he walks in from the room's clear side first (`walk=left|right` to choose).
- With no `{pose}` written, poses are picked from the words by the pose tags (a pose every six words at most).

The render (remotion/src/paper/setpiece.tsx) crops the room for the frame, lightens it and boils it on 12 drawings
a second, keeps the push-in and a shake on a shock on the same clock, and pins the cards outside the push so they
stay still. A Short with set pieces carries its captions high, on a white pill. The ones planned, and what each still

### Tricks (code, no new drawings)

- Treadmill walk: he walks on the spot while a long street scrolls behind him, for journeys and "years later".
- Pan across a wide background while he walks, the camera following.
- Props pop in on the word (keys, a £20 note, a payslip) with the same squash and stretch.
- Speech and thought bubbles drawn on; a thought bubble can hold a prop or a background.
- Punch-in: a cut to his head and shoulders from any pose for a punchline.
- Screen shake on shock; motion lines on a fast exit.
- Foreground objects slide past faster than the background (depth).
- Montage: a background changes behind a held pose, on the beat (graduation, first job, first flat, keys).
- Scene change: the next page slides over, or an iris closes on him.
