# How Jeffrey talks (write scripts like this)

Jeffrey, 11 November 2026, on a script that read like an article: "More like me. Talk more like me." The reference is
his own unscripted recording, projects/first-10k (episode 1 of Road to £100K). A script is him talking to one person,
not a presenter reading.

## What his talking sounds like
- Starts thoughts with "So": "So here's what happened", "So let me take you back", "So here's how I'm going to do it".
- Short plain sentences, joined with "and". Says a thing, then says it again shorter: "That's it." "It really wasn't."
- Honest asides: "I'll be up front", "I'm going to be honest", "honestly", "to be fair", "here's the thing".
- Checks in with the viewer: "right?" at the end of a point (once or twice a video, not every line), "you know".
- Says "grand" for thousands ("10 grand", "11 grand"), "100k". Never "quid".
- Everyday words: "pretty much", "a bit more", "kind of", "the guy who runs", "wreck", "way more".
- Owns his mistakes: "I did almost everything wrong", "that was my biggest mistake", "I haven't learned my lesson".
- A normal person, not a finance guy: "I'm not a financial advisor", "I'm pretty much a normal person".
- Says where he stands and why: what he holds, what he is doing, what he would not do again.
- Signs off: "tell me in the comments", "I'll see you in the next one". After the hook: "I'm Jeffrey, and this is The
  Everyday Investor."

## What he has said on camera about his own money (safe to build on; confirm anything newer with him)
- The core is the S&P 500: "it's boring, it's broad, but it does all the heavy lifting".
- About 20 to 30% goes into individual stocks: "that's me being a bit more aggressive, because I'm on the younger side
  and I don't have any people depending on me". About 5% into crypto.
- An emergency fund, kept away from investments. Only invest what you can afford to lose. Have a plan.
- His story: about £3,000 became £11,000 in crypto after Covid, slid to £4,500, and he sold out of emotion.
- Background: working class, first in his family to go to university and did not finish, a software bootcamp, two
  years at a private bank, now a consultant; works with AI. Saving for a first home. Road to £100K.
- No balances or holdings values on screen. A new claim about what he holds or has done goes in [BRACKETS] until he
  confirms it.

## Keeping it him without overdoing it
- Light touch on fillers: one "you know", one or two "right?", two or three "honestly" or "to be fair" in a video. The
  made voice reads every word, and a filler in every line sounds fake.
- Facts stay exact while the wording relaxes: figures, dates, "admitted" against "alleged", and sources do not loosen.
- Read each line aloud: if it sounds like an article, it is not him yet.
