# How much do you need to be in the top 10% in the UK? Research, checked 3 November 2026

gov.uk and ons.gov.uk are blocked in the build session, so figures were checked through web search against
secondary pages that cite the tables. Confidence noted for each. Check the primary tables before recording.

| Figure | Value | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top 10% income, before tax | £67,400+ | HMRC Survey of Personal Incomes 2023-24, Table 3.1a, published April 2026 | High: several sources agree |
| Top 5% | about £93,600 to £93,700 | same | Medium: sources differ by £100 |
| Top 1% | £207,000+ before tax, £134,000 after | same | High |
| Median | £29,700 | same | High |
| Top 10% take-home | £4,137 a month | worked from 2026-27 England rates, no pension or student loan | Exact for those assumptions; Scotland differs |
| Top 10% full-time pay by age | 22-29 £52,197; 30-39 £75,041; 40-49 £88,658; 50-59 £85,226 | ONS ASHE 2025 provisional, Table 6.7a (via ukpersonal.finance) | Medium: ASHE 2026 provisional came out late Oct 2026 and revises 2025; use the newer table |
| Top 10% household wealth | £1,200,500+ | ONS Wealth and Assets Survey Apr 2020-Mar 2022 (bulletin 24 Jan 2025), via House of Commons Library | High |
| Top 1% household wealth | £3,121,500+ | same survey, secondary source only | Medium |
| Median household wealth | £293,700 incl. pensions; £181,700 without | same, House of Commons Library | High |

Caveats to say on screen or in the description:
- HMRC covers income taxpayers only, about 60% of UK adults, and counts all taxable income, not salary. The top
  10% of all adults would be a lower bar. Say "of taxpayers".
- Income and wealth are different tops: you can be top 10% by pay and nowhere near top 10% by wealth.
- The wealth survey is household, Great Britain only, and the Commons Library notes it is less reliable since Covid.

Take-home at each line (2026-27, England, no pension or student loan): £29,700 is £2,075 a month; £52,197 is
£3,403; £67,400 is £4,137; £75,041 is £4,507; £88,658 is £5,165; £207,000 is about £10,125.
