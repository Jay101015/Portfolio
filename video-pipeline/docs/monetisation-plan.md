# Monetisation plan: animated finance and investing channel

A roadmap from zero to a channel that pays, for an illustrated host who
explains money, built on the pipeline in this repo. Numbers are planning
estimates, not promises. Every figure marked "range" is the spread reported
across finance and investing channels in 2026; your audience mix (country,
age, how many of them actually open accounts) moves you inside it.

## 1. What the business actually is

A finance explainer channel earns from five things, in the order they usually
arrive:

| Stream | Needs | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube ads (AdSense) | Partner Program | $9 to $30 per 1,000 long-form views (RPM) | Five to eight times entertainment. US, UK, Canadian and Australian viewers set the top of the range; India and South-East Asia the bottom. |
| Shorts ads | Partner Program | $0.05 to $0.25 per 1,000 Shorts views | Finance Shorts pay more than most, and still a rounding error. Shorts are the funnel. |
| Memberships and Patreon | 500 subs (fan funding tier) | $5 to $15 per member per month | Converts 0.5% to 2% of an engaged audience. A spreadsheet, a calculator or a monthly Q&A is the perk that converts. |
| Sponsorships | About 10,000 average views per video | $20 to $55 per 1,000 views for a 60-second read; $75 to $200 claimed for strong investing channels | The main line. Brokerages, budgeting apps, credit tools, insurers and tax software all bid for the same viewers. Dedicated videos pay two to four times a read. |
| Affiliates and products | A recognisable host | Highly variable | Brokerage and savings-account signups pay per account. A budgeting template or a course is the classic second product. |

Two things that quietly decide revenue:

- **Trust.** A finance sponsor measures you by the accounts its viewers open,
  not by views. A channel that is careful with numbers, shows its sources on
  screen and never oversells keeps sponsors for years. One misleading
  thumbnail costs more than it earns.
- **Advertiser suitability.** YouTube treats finance as a sensitive category.
  Get-rich-quick framing, crypto hype, "guaranteed returns", gambling
  adjacency and undisclosed promotions get limited ads or strikes. Explain,
  compare, show the maths, disclose every paid mention.

## 2. Eligibility milestones

| Milestone | Threshold | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| Fan funding tier | 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, and 3,000 public watch hours in 12 months (or 3M Shorts views in 90 days) | Channel memberships, Super Thanks, Super Chat, shopping |
| Full Partner Program | 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months (or 10M Shorts views in 90 days) | Ad revenue on long-form and Shorts |

Watch hours is the real gate. An 8-minute video watched 50% of the way is 4
minutes of watch time, so 4,000 hours is about 60,000 half-watched views.
Videos over 8 minutes also unlock mid-roll ads, which is why every script in
this plan is written to 8 to 12 minutes.

## 3. Revenue model by stage

Monthly figures. Assumes 8 long-form videos a month at the views shown, 50%
average view duration, mid-rolls on, and the RPM range above. Sponsorships
assume one read per two videos once eligible, at the low end of the range
until a sponsor renews.

| Stage | Avg views per video | Ads | Members | Sponsors | Total per month |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-YPP (months 1 to 5) | 300 to 1,500 | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Just monetised | 1,500 to 4,000 | $100 to $900 | $0 to $100 | $0 | $100 to $1,000 |
| 10k average views | 10,000 | $700 to $2,400 | $250 to $1,000 | $800 to $2,200 | $1,800 to $5,600 |
| 30k average views | 30,000 | $2,200 to $7,200 | $750 to $3,000 | $2,400 to $6,600 | $5,400 to $16,800 |
| 100k average views | 100,000 | $7,200 to $24,000 | $2,500 to $10,000 | $8,000 to $40,000 | $17,700 to $74,000 |

Read it the right way around: in finance the ad line is real money, unlike in
entertainment, but sponsors still win once the channel is trusted, and the
trust is built by the videos, not the view count.

## 4. Formats that fit an illustrated host

The host is an explainer, not an investor showing a portfolio. That is the
positioning: "a cartoon that shows you where the money actually goes." Three
series, each reusing the pipeline's stat, chart, compare and list scenes:

| Series | What it is | Why it pays |
|---|---|---|
| **One number** | One figure, taken apart in 8 minutes: the 1% fee, the 4% rule, the 72 rule, the average car payment | Evergreen search traffic; the chart scenes do the work; easiest to sponsor with brokerages and budgeting apps |
| **Where the money goes** | The economics of a thing people buy or watch: a movie, a concert tour, a streaming service, a sneaker, a flight | Pulls business and finance advertisers, less crowded than personal-finance basics, and the drawn host can show the flow of money visually |
| **How it actually works** | A product or a scam explained: index funds, ETFs, credit scores, buy-now-pay-later, 0% car finance, annuities | High trust, high retention, the videos that get a channel recommended |

Avoid: stock picks, price targets, crypto calls, "I made $X this month". They
pay once and cost the channel its advertiser suitability and its credibility.

## 5. How to get there

### Phase 0: before upload one (two weeks)

- Lock the host on the pose sheet. Once it is on a thumbnail it is the brand.
- Make three videos before publishing any, one from each series above. The
  demo in `projects/fee-drag` is the first "One number" episode.
- Write every script with a source line under every number. The stat and
  chart scenes carry a `source=` attribute for this reason; fill it in.
- A standing disclaimer in every outro: education, not advice. Say it, do not
  bury it in the description.
- Record the voice properly. One condenser mic, a quiet room, a consistent
  distance. The voice is the trust signal.

### Phase 1: 0 to 1,000 subscribers (months 1 to 5)

- Cadence: two long-form videos a week, 8 to 12 minutes, plus three Shorts a
  week cut from them with the vertical render (one number, one chart, 40
  seconds).
- Titles are questions people type: "Is a 1% fee a lot?", "What does an index
  fund actually do?", "Where does the money from a movie ticket go?" Search
  is the growth engine here, not trends.
- Thumbnails: the host's reaction face, one big number, four words or fewer.
- Measure click-through rate (aim above 5%) and average view duration (aim
  above 50%). Fix the first with thumbnails, the second with tighter openings:
  state the number in the first ten seconds.
- Apply for the fan funding tier at 500 subscribers and switch memberships on
  the same day with one perk: the spreadsheet behind each video.

### Phase 2: 1,000 to 10,000 (months 5 to 12)

- Partner Program on. Mid-rolls at scene cuts only, never mid-sentence.
- Pitch the first sponsors yourself at around 5,000 average views: a
  one-page media kit from the analytics, a US-audience percentage, and your
  rule that you only take products you would explain anyway. Price at $25 per
  1,000 expected views and let them negotiate.
- Affiliate links for brokerages and savings accounts in every description,
  disclosed in the video.
- Community polls for the next number. Cheap engagement, keeps returning
  viewers up.

### Phase 3: 10,000 to 50,000 (months 12 to 24)

- Sponsorships become the main line. One read per video, after the first
  scene, inside the drawn frame so it feels like part of the show. Join a
  creator marketplace once inbound asks start.
- First product: a budgeting template or fee-finder spreadsheet, priced low,
  sold to the audience that asked for it.
- A second format that reuses everything: a 30-minute podcast cut of two
  episodes with the host on screen the whole time.
- Hire a researcher with sponsor money. Your time goes to scripts and voice.

### Phase 4: 50,000 and up

- A second channel from the same pipeline: Shorts-only explainers, or a
  different money topic (small business, housing, careers).
- Direct brand deals, a newsletter with affiliate links, and a course only
  if the audience has asked for one by name.

## 6. Weekly operating rhythm

| Day | Work |
|---|---|
| Mon | Pick two numbers or topics. Research with sources. Write both scripts with scene tags, source lines and reaction cues. |
| Tue | Record both voiceovers. Run the build with `--no-render`, review the storyboard pages, fix cues. |
| Wed | Render both. Cut three Shorts from the vertical renders. Make thumbnails from reaction stills. |
| Thu | Publish video one. Reply to every comment for the first two hours. |
| Fri | Publish video two. Schedule Shorts for Sat, Sun, Mon. |
| Sat | Analytics: CTR, average view duration, RPM per video. One change to next week's scripts based on it. |

The pipeline makes this a two-person-hour-per-video operation once the
scripts exist. The research, the scripts and the voice are the job.

## 7. Compliance, the short version

- **Disclose every paid mention**, spoken and on screen, at the moment it
  starts. The FTC and YouTube's paid-promotion checkbox both apply.
- **No personal advice.** "Here is how it works" and "here is the maths",
  never "you should buy". The outro disclaimer is a habit, not a shield.
- **Source every number on screen.** It is also what makes the channel
  quotable, which is how finance channels grow.
- **Affiliate links** are paid mentions. Say so.
- If you ever hold or are compensated by something you discuss, say so in
  the video.

## 8. Risks and the answer to each

| Risk | Answer |
|---|---|
| Inauthentic content policy (mass-produced, AI-voiced videos) | Your own voice, your own research, a drawn host with reactions written per video. Keep the output varied: different scene mixes, different cue patterns, real sources. |
| A wrong number | A source line under every figure, a research pass before recording, a pinned correction comment when it happens. Credibility survives corrections, not silence. |
| Advertiser suitability | No hype, no guarantees, no crypto calls, no gambling. Thumbnails state a fact, not a promise. |
| Slower growth than entertainment | Search-led titles, evergreen topics, series that compound. Expect months 1 to 5 to be quiet and month 12 to be steady. |
| Sponsor dependence | Keep ads, members, affiliates and a product each above 10% of revenue by phase 3. |
| Burnout on a twice-weekly cadence | The pipeline removes editing. Batch record. If a week slips, publish one, never zero. |

## 9. What to track

One sheet, updated weekly: subscribers, watch hours toward 4,000, CTR,
average view duration, returning viewers, RPM per video, US-audience share,
member count, sponsor revenue. If CTR and average view duration are both
rising, everything else follows.
