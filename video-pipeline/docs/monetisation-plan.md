# Monetisation plan: animated movie-review channel

A roadmap from zero to a channel that pays, for a hand-drawn host character
reviewing films, built on the pipeline in this repo. Numbers are planning
estimates, not promises. Every figure marked "range" is the spread seen across
entertainment channels of the same shape; your audience mix (country, age,
watch device) moves you inside it.

## 1. What the business actually is

An animated review channel earns from five things, in the order they usually
arrive:

| Stream | Needs | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube ads (AdSense) | Partner Program | $1.50 to $4.50 per 1,000 long-form views (RPM) | Film and entertainment commentary sits well below finance and tech. US and UK heavy audiences are at the top of the range. |
| Shorts ads | Partner Program | $0.03 to $0.10 per 1,000 Shorts views | Tiny per view. Shorts are a discovery engine, not a revenue line. |
| Memberships and Patreon | 500 subs (fan funding tier) | $3 to $8 per member per month | Converts 0.5% to 2% of an engaged audience. Animated channels convert above average because the character is a brand. |
| Sponsorships | About 10,000 average views per video | $15 to $35 per 1,000 views for a 60-second integrated read | The largest line once it starts. Typical first sponsors for this niche: VPNs, streaming aggregators, meal kits, mobile games, poster and art-print shops. |
| Merch and the character | A recognisable character | Highly variable | The one advantage a cartoon host has over a face channel: the host is a product. Stickers, prints, plush, a Discord role. |

Two things that quietly decide revenue:

- **Content ID claims.** A claim does not remove the video. It moves the ad
  revenue to the claimant. A review channel whose ad money goes to studios is
  a hobby. Public-domain films and short trailer or still cutaways keep the
  money.
- **Advertiser suitability.** Horror gore, strong language in the first 30
  seconds, and thumbnails with blood get limited ads. Review the film, show
  the scary moment in the drawn frame, keep the opening clean.

## 2. Eligibility milestones

| Milestone | Threshold | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| Fan funding tier | 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, and 3,000 public watch hours in 12 months (or 3M Shorts views in 90 days) | Channel memberships, Super Thanks, Super Chat, shopping |
| Full Partner Program | 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months (or 10M Shorts views in 90 days) | Ad revenue on long-form and Shorts |

Watch hours is the real gate. A 6-minute video watched 50% of the way is
3 minutes of watch time, so 4,000 hours is about 80,000 half-watched views.
That is why the plan below leans on long-form with Shorts as the funnel.

## 3. Revenue model by stage

Monthly figures. Assumes 8 long-form videos a month at the views shown, 50%
average view duration, and the RPM range above. Sponsorships assume one read
per two videos once eligible.

| Stage | Avg views per video | Ads | Members | Sponsors | Total per month |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-YPP (months 1 to 4) | 500 to 2,000 | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Just monetised | 2,000 to 5,000 | $25 to $180 | $0 to $60 | $0 | $25 to $240 |
| 10k average views | 10,000 | $120 to $360 | $150 to $500 | $600 to $1,400 | $900 to $2,300 |
| 30k average views | 30,000 | $360 to $1,100 | $450 to $1,500 | $1,800 to $4,200 | $2,600 to $6,800 |
| 100k average views | 100,000 | $1,200 to $3,600 | $1,500 to $5,000 | $6,000 to $14,000 | $8,700 to $22,600 |

Read the table the right way around: ads are the floor, sponsorships and
members are the business, and both of those depend on the character being
liked, not on view count alone.

## 4. How to get there

### Phase 0: before upload one (two weeks)

- Lock the character on the pose sheet. Once it is on a thumbnail, it is the
  brand. Do not redesign after launch.
- Make three videos before publishing any. One review of a public-domain
  classic, one review of a current release using trailer cutaways only, one
  "the money behind the movie" piece. The third format uses the chart and
  stat scenes and pulls in business-adjacent advertisers, which lift RPM.
- Channel art, a 45-second trailer, a one-line positioning: "a cartoon critic
  who tells you whether it is worth your evening."
- Record the voice properly. One condenser mic, a quiet room, a consistent
  distance. The voice is the authenticity signal YouTube's policy team and
  the audience both key on.

### Phase 1: 0 to 1,000 subscribers (months 1 to 4)

- Cadence: two long-form videos a week, 6 to 8 minutes, plus three Shorts a
  week cut from them with the vertical render. Consistency beats quality
  spikes at this stage.
- Series over one-offs. "Free to watch tonight" (public domain), "Worth your
  ticket?" (current releases), "Why it flopped" (money behind the movie). A
  series gives a returning viewer a reason to come back and gives the
  algorithm a pattern.
- Thumbnails: the character's reaction face, large, with four words or
  fewer. Animated channels win on thumbnails because the face is always
  available and always on-model. Build a thumbnail still into every script
  with a `[still]` reaction frame.
- Titles: the film plus a verdict or a question. "Night of the Living Dead is
  still the blueprint." "Is the new Alien worth the ticket?"
- Measure two numbers only: click-through rate (aim above 5%) and average
  view duration (aim above 50%). Fix the first with thumbnails, the second
  with tighter openings. Cut every intro to under 10 seconds.
- Apply for the fan funding tier at 500 subscribers and switch memberships
  on the same day, with one perk: early access and a say in the next review.

### Phase 2: 1,000 to 10,000 (months 4 to 10)

- Partner Program on. Enable mid-roll ads only on videos over 8 minutes and
  place them at scene cuts, never mid-sentence.
- Community tab polls for the next film. Cheap engagement, keeps the
  returning-viewer rate up.
- First collaborations: trade guest spots with two other animated or review
  channels of similar size. The character can appear in theirs as a drawn
  cameo, which costs you one pose sheet.
- Start a Patreon only if members on YouTube pass 50. Otherwise it splits a
  small audience.
- Pitch the first sponsors yourself at around 5,000 average views. Make a
  one-page media kit from the channel analytics. Price at $20 per 1,000
  expected views and let them negotiate down.

### Phase 3: 10,000 to 50,000 (months 10 to 20)

- Sponsorships become the main line. Join a creator marketplace or an agency
  once inbound asks start, and keep reads to one per video, after the first
  scene, inside the drawn frame so it feels like part of the show.
- Merch with the character: a small print-on-demand run, stickers and a
  print first. Treat the first run as a test of whether people want the
  character, not as revenue.
- A second format that reuses everything: a 30-minute podcast cut of two
  reviews with the host on screen the whole time. Zero extra animation cost.
- Hire an editor or script assistant with sponsor money. Your time goes to
  scripts and voice, which are the two things nobody else can do.

### Phase 4: 50,000 and up

- License the character into a second channel (animated Shorts-only reactions,
  or a non-film topic) run by the same pipeline.
- Direct brand deals, a newsletter with affiliate links to the films being
  discussed, and live watch-alongs for members.

## 5. Weekly operating rhythm

| Day | Work |
|---|---|
| Mon | Pick two films. Research. Write both scripts with scene tags and reaction cues. |
| Tue | Record both voiceovers. Run the build with `--no-render`, review the storyboard pages, fix cues. |
| Wed | Render both. Cut three Shorts from the vertical renders. Make thumbnails from reaction stills. |
| Thu | Publish video one. Reply to every comment for the first two hours. |
| Fri | Publish video two. Schedule Shorts for Sat, Sun, Mon. |
| Sat | Analytics: CTR and average view duration per video. One change to next week's scripts based on it. |

The pipeline makes this a two-person-hour-per-video operation once the
scripts exist. The scripts and the voice are the job.

## 6. Risks and the answer to each

| Risk | Answer |
|---|---|
| Inauthentic content policy (July 2025 update targets mass-produced, repetitive, AI-voiced videos) | Your own voice, your own opinions, a drawn character with reactions written per video. Keep the pipeline's output varied: different scene mixes, different cue patterns, real footage choices. |
| Content ID claims eating revenue | Public-domain titles for long clips. Trailers and stills for current films, under 10 seconds each, inside the frame, with commentary over them. Dispute claims on clear fair-use commentary; most studios release on dispute for review content. |
| Low RPM niche | Mix in the "money behind the movie" format and film-business explainers, which attract finance and tech advertisers. Sponsorships and members do the heavy lifting regardless. |
| Burnout on a twice-weekly cadence | The pipeline removes editing. Batch record. If a week slips, publish one, never zero. |
| Character fatigue | Add one new pose or prop every month. Seasonal outfits. The audience should see the host evolve. |

## 7. What to track

One sheet, updated weekly: subscribers, watch hours toward 4,000, CTR,
average view duration, returning viewers, member count, sponsor revenue. If
CTR and average view duration are both rising, everything else follows.
