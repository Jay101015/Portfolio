# What the UK finance channels do (researched 2026-10-31)

YouTube and the analytics sites cannot be fetched from the cloud session, so these figures come from search-engine
summaries of OutlierKit's channel pages and press coverage; treat them as estimates and check them in YouTube.

## British Finance with Jack (@BritishFinancewithJack)
- About six months old; about 10.8K subscribers, 1.3M views, posting about six videos a week; estimated $488 to
  $2K a month. Audience UK, 25 to 44, mostly men. Faceless, template scripts, public benchmarks, stock visuals.
- Topics: wealth, class and net-worth benchmarking ("anxiety-driven financial benchmarking and reality checks for
  the British middle class"); UK tax, SIPP and pension policy changes.
- Top video: "Why Everything Changes After You Save £20,000 in the UK?", about 138K views, about 46 times the
  channel's typical video (so a typical video is about 3K). Follow-up: "Why Everything Changes After You Save Your
  First £10,000 In The UK".
- The title is a localisation of a proven format: Nischa's "ACCOUNTANT EXPLAINS: Why Everything Changes After
  $20K" (about 2.5M), Graham Stephan's "Why EVERYTHING Changes After You Save $10K", Australian copies. Jack adds
  "in the UK" and posts the same promise over and over; one outlier carries the channel.

## Damien Talks Money (the UK benchmark)
- About 407K subscribers, 35.8M views over 268 uploads. Top video: "Signs You're Doing Well Financially (Even If
  It Doesn't Feel Like It)", about 1.2M. Face on camera, own voice, ISAs, pensions, FIRE.
- Same genre at the top: the viewer measuring themselves against everyone else, with reassurance.

## What the successful ones share (and what The Everyday Investor does not yet do)
1. The title names a milestone the viewer has or wants (£10,000, £20,000, "doing well") and says "UK".
2. The promise is about the viewer's standing: are you ahead, behind, normal. Not a story, not a lesson.
3. The same promise repeated as a series (the £10K video, the £20K video, the £50K video).
4. Volume: many cheap shots at one outlier. Jack's typical video does about 3K; the title made the 138K, not
   the production.

## What to take and what to leave
- Take the title grammar, not the production: "Why everything changes after your first £10,000 (UK)" with
  Jeffrey's own balance in it is the same search and browse hook with the one thing Jack cannot do.
- Put "UK" in the title and "in the UK" in the first line of the description on every video.
- A benchmarking series: "Signs you're doing well with money at 25 (UK)", "What £30K a year actually gets you",
  "How much should you have saved by 25 / 30", one a month, each with his own number as the personal beat.
- Volume through Shorts cut from the long videos and the Shorts-first lane, not six long videos a week.
- Leave the AI voice and the stock-clip wallpaper: the retention is where that format loses, and the channel's
  own voice, own numbers and the drawn look are the moat. Check this against the retention graphs once vidIQ
  credits return (27 Nov): pull Jack's £20K video stats and compare average view duration if visible.
